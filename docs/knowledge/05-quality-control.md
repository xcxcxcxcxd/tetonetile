# Quality control and lots

Handmade zellige **varies by nature** - in colour, size, thickness and surface. Quality control does not look for perfect tiles; it checks that a batch stays **within the standard we publish** (Material Specification §06) and **matches the colour the client approved**.

## The Variation & Imperfection standard

| Characteristic | Scale | Glazed | Dark glazes* | Natural clay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colour variation | 1 low · 2 slight · 3 medium · 4 heavy · 5 extreme | ≤ 3 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 2 |
| Texture variation | 1 smooth · 2 slight · 3 medium · 4 heavy | ≤ 3 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 3 |
| Size variation | 1 slight · 2 medium · 3 heavy · 4 extreme | ≤ 3 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 3 |
| Pits / chips | 1 will occur · 2 prevalent · 3 heavy | ≤ 2 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 2 |
| Cracks / crazing | 1 will occur · 2 prevalent · 3 heavy | ≤ 2 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 1 |
| Iron spots | 1 will occur · 2 prevalent · 3 heavy | ≤ 1 | ≤ 1 | ≤ 1 |

\* Dark and heavily pigmented glazes may be rated **one level higher** for pits, chips and crazing (for example 39 Rabat black). The system applies this automatically from the colour.

Pits and chips are more frequent on bejmat than on squares, and more visible in dark glazes. About 15–20 % of pieces show some crazing, chips or pits - that is normal and is why we sell with overage.

## At the sorting table (Sorting & QC stage)

1. Scan the production card. The shop-floor screen opens on **Sorting & QC**.
2. Sort every piece into **Grade A** (first choice), **Grade B** (second choice, sold separately) or **Reject**. Enter the three counts.
3. Take a **sample of the batch** and tap the level you see on each scale. Levels above the standard show dashed; choosing one warns that the lot will be held.
4. Compare with the **approved colour reference** (the sample board or the pieces the client approved). If it does not match, tap **No - hold the lot**.
5. Enter the inspector's name and **Save**.

If every level is within the standard and the colour matches, the batch moves on to packing. If not, the lot goes on **QC hold**:

- the inspection is recorded with the levels that were exceeded;
- the lot shows on [Quality & lots](/production/qc) and on the dashboard;
- work can continue, but **packed pallets go to the quarantine zone** (Q) and are **not reserved** for the client's order.

## Deciding on a held lot

The production manager (or owner) opens the lot and decides:

| Decision | What to do in the system |
|---|---|
| **Acceptable after all** (within tolerance on a second look, client agrees in writing) | **Release lot**, with the reason. Pallets leave quarantine and are reserved for the order automatically |
| **Not for this client, fine for others** | Release it, then on the order click **unreserve** on that line - the pallets become free stock for other clients - and produce a new lot for the order (**Release to production** again, or a New MO) |
| **Remake** | Keep it on hold, create a new MO for the order. Never mix the held lot into the order |

Any lot can also be put **on hold** by hand (**Put on hold** on the lot page) - for example when a client claim reveals a problem. Every in-stock pallet of the lot is blocked at once.

## Lot traceability

Scan any carton or pallet label, or open a lot from [Quality & lots](/production/qc?tab=lots). The lot page answers *"where did this batch come from and where did it go?"*:

- the MO and the order it was made for;
- every stage with dates, good pieces and scrap;
- the **kiln loads** it was fired in, with temperature and fuel;
- the QC inspections and their levels;
- every pallet and box group, where it is now, which order it is reserved for and which shipment took it;
- claims linked to the lot.

The same records are printed as the **batch production record** for US origin files (Shipment → Origin file).

## Grade B and rejects

- Grade B is packed separately (its own pallets, *grade B* on the label) and never mixed into a grade A order.
- Rejects are counted as scrap at sorting. Broken pieces can be kept for mosaics and repairs, but they leave the stock count.

## Why it matters

A claim is judged against the standard, not against a machine-made tile. The QC record of the lot - levels, inspector, date, approved reference - is what lets us answer a claim with facts. See [Claims](kb:claims).
